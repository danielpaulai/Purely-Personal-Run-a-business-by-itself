# The 5 Emails That Get Clients

Five shapes. Fill the slots, read it out loud, send it. Every example below is real or clearly marked as an example.

---

## 1. Client Win

**What it does:** shows your work is real, without bragging. The client is the hero. You are the guide.
**How often:** once a week is a great rhythm. "Win Wednesday."
**Length:** 120 to 200 words.

**The shape**
1. Subject: the result.
2. "I just…" and how you heard (a message, a call, a post).
3. Where they were before. The problem, in numbers if you can.
4. What they did. Use the names of your method or program parts.
5. Where they are now.
6. "If you want help with this, …" and your one ask.

**Real example (Danny's list)**

> **Subject:** Month one of the Circle
>
> Hey [first name],
>
> Our AI Founder Circle Cohort 01 just finished their first month.
>
> 30 days ago, most of them had real skills and no clear way to turn them into a business that pays them.
>
> After month one, they have their offer locked and their Second Brain built, so their AI team knows their business as well as they do.
>
> Content goes out from day one, instead of waiting until they feel ready.
>
> And they have learned how to start conversations and close on a sales call.
>
> Now they are doubling down on the part that pays: booking calls and closing them.
>
> If you want help building this for your business, reply "Circle" and I'll send you the details.
>
> Danny

**No clients yet?** Use someone you helped for free, a friend, a beta tester, or your own result. Say so honestly: "Last week I helped a friend who runs a bakery…" Never make one up.

**Ask before you name a client.** Or describe them: "a bookkeeper in Leeds."

## 2. Story

**What it does:** people remember stories, and they get to know you. Most people's days are ordinary. Let them enjoy yours.
**How often:** once a week.
**Length:** 200 to 350 words.

**The shape**
1. Start in the moment: where you were, when.
2. Tell it in order.
3. Long story? Add one line that keeps them reading: "Stay with me, the ending is the point."
4. The lesson, tied to their world.
5. One small next step.

**Where stories come from:** your phone photos, a client call, something your kid said, a trip, a mistake, a meal, a film you watched.

## 3. The 9-Word Email

**What it does:** a tiny email that gets replies. It wakes up a quiet list and tells you who is ready to talk.
**How often:** every 2 to 3 weeks. Send it on a Monday morning so replies land on working days. Change the script each time.
**Length:** 10 to 25 words. Short enough to read in three seconds.

**The shape: who, result, reply**
- Subject: their first name, or one plain word.
- Who you want to hear from, and the result they want. Every script names the who, even the questions.
- Preview text: one real fact that gives a reason to open, never a line about replying.
- One word to reply with, in quotes.
- Your first name.

No link. No PS. The reply is the ask. See `nine-word-scripts.md` for the seven scripts.

**Example**

> **Subject:** {{first_name}}
>
> I'm looking to work with a few founders who want their first $8,000 from their business. Reply "yes" if that's you.
>
> Danny

**Never:** a question with no result in it ("Do you run a solo business?"). Nobody knows why you're asking, so nobody replies.

**When they reply:** answer like a person, then invite them to a short call.

## 4. How-To

**What it does:** teaches one useful thing. It builds trust, and proves you know your stuff.
**How often:** once a week.
**Length:** 280 to 380 words.

**The shape**
1. The result they want.
2. Why it is hard. The roadblock most people hit.
3. Three tips. A short label and two to four sentences each.
4. One invitation.

**Easy source:** anything you already teach. A workshop, a call, a post that did well. One section becomes one email.

## 5. Event Invite (three emails)

**What it does:** fills a workshop, webinar or call without a sales page.
**How often:** every event.

**Email 1 · The invite** (under 40 words)
> **Subject:** {{first_name}}
>
> I'm running a free workshop for [people] on [topic] on [date]. Would you like to join us?
>
> Danny

**Email 2 · The details** (send to everyone who replies "tell me more", 150 to 250 words)
> Thanks for getting back to me.
>
> Here is what we are doing. [What it is, who it is for.]
>
> You will leave with: [two or three concrete things].
>
> It's on [day, date, time, place]. [The price, if there is one.]
>
> Just hit reply and say "I'm in" and I'll save your spot.
>
> Danny

**Email 3 · The check in** (three days later, to people who did not reply)
> Are you still thinking about joining us on [date]?
>
> Danny

**Real example of the idea in action (Danny's list):** in July, Danny opened 40 seats for a live workshop. More than 60 people registered within five days and the doors closed early.

## Plus: The 8-Minute Email

Not a template, a habit.
1. Pick one idea.
2. First line: "I'm just going to type what comes into my head."
3. Type for 8 minutes. No delete key. No going back.
4. Leave it. The next day, spend 8 minutes polishing it: one idea, the payoff line, read it out loud, a better first line, three subject lines.
