# The Email Rules

## The 4 rules

1. **Send often.** Pick a number of emails a week for the next 30 days. One is fine. Every step up means more people helped.
2. **Every email helps.** A tip, a story, a laugh. The reader should be glad they opened it, so they open the next one.
3. **Every email asks for one small thing.** Reply, click, book, join. One ask, not two.
4. **Sound like you.** Not like a brand, not like AI. Like you at your best.

## How to write

- Plain text. Write like a note to one person.
- One idea per email. Save the second idea for next week.
- Short paragraphs, one to three sentences. People read on phones.
- Start with the most interesting part. No warm up.
- Use real names, real numbers, real days. Never invent any of them.
- Name the old way and the better way.
- Land one line they'll remember.
- Read it out loud. Wherever you stumble, rewrite it the way you'd say it.
- Add a PS with three ways you can help, smallest to biggest.
- Write in the language your clients read.
- Send at 80 percent. It gets better every week.

## The coffee test

Would you say it to a client over coffee? If not, don't write it.

## Who you can email

- **Your newsletter:** only people who said yes. They signed up, or they replied yes when you asked.
- **People you know who never signed up:** send them one personal email first and ask. "I'm starting a short weekly email about [topic]. Want me to add you?"
- **Every newsletter email** has an unsubscribe link and your business address. GoHighLevel, Mailchimp and ActiveCampaign add both for you. A business address or PO box is fine.
- **Never** buy a list or copy one from somewhere else.
- **Ask before naming a client** in an email.

*This is a plain rule of thumb, not legal advice.*

## Your first unsubscribe

It will happen. It's a good sign. The people who leave were never going to buy, and the ones who stay want to hear from you.

## What to measure

Count four things every Friday: **emails sent, replies, new subscribers, calls booked.**
Open rates are unreliable now, so only compare them with your own past numbers.
