# Subject Lines

The subject line decides if your email gets read. The best ones are **2 to 6 words**. Write three for every email, then pick one.

## The rules

- **2 to 6 words.** Under 45 characters, and the first 33 must make sense on their own. That's all the Gmail phone app shows.
- Write like a person: sentence case, or all lowercase if that's you. Never Title Case.
- No exclamation marks. No ALL CAPS. No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- No clickbait the email doesn't pay off.
- One emoji at most, at the end, and rarely.
- Invites and reminders can be boring on purpose: "Invitation", "Doors close tonight".

## Subject + preview = one line

The grey preview text after the subject is the second half of the same thought. Two ways to do it:

- **Second punch:** a short line that flips the angle.
  - Subject: *Done beats perfect* · Preview: *Perfect is where ideas go to hide*
- **The payoff:** one sentence that says what's inside.
  - Subject: *What IKEA knows about onboarding* · Preview: *Make the first 10 minutes so easy nobody quits.*

Never repeat the subject, and never put "just hit reply" in the preview.

## The 12 patterns

Pick one. Each has a fill-in shape and examples.

**1. Odd pair.** Two things that don't belong together.
*[thing] and [thing]* · Spreadsheets and sourdough · My daughter and my laptop · Receipts and rollercoasters

**2. A character.** One person in one scene.
*The [person] in [place]* · The founder in the front row · The client who cried on Tuesday · The baker with three laptops

**3. Confession.** You admit something.
*I [did the wrong thing]* · I was the bottleneck · I've been undercharging · I closed the doors early · I wasted a month on this

**4. A number that stings.** One specific, surprising number.
*[number] [thing], [number] [result]* · 60 people, 40 seats · Two years, zero leads · 400 pounds in a shoebox

**5. Famous name + your business.** Something everyone knows, applied to your world.
*What [famous thing] knows about [your topic]* · What IKEA knows about onboarding · Don't copy Apple · The Lego lesson in pricing

**6. Short maxim.** A rule they could put on a sticky note.
*[rule]* · Done beats perfect · Hope is not a plan · Send it at 80 percent · Slow is smooth

**7. Their words.** A question or objection, in quotes.
*"[what they say]"* · "I'm not technical enough" · "Let's talk in January" · "I can't afford it right now"

**8. Callout.** Name who it's for, or who won't like it.
*If you [are/do X], read this* · If you hate selling, read this · Accountants will hate this one

**9. Warning.** Something they're missing.
*[Who] notice this before you do* · Your clients notice this first · you missed something · Why people leave your page

**10. Life moment.** Something small from your week.
*In my [thing] era / I [did a small thing]* · In my school-run era · I got a haircut · My flight got cancelled

**11. Question.** One short question they want to answer.
*[question]?* · Take next week off? · One question before Friday · Still doing the books yourself?

**12. Plain label.** For invites, reminders and logistics. Boring on purpose.
*[what it is]* · Invitation · Workshop invitation · Doors close tonight · Starting in 2 hours · {{first_name}}, missed you today

## Which pattern for which email

| Email | Try first |
|---|---|
| Client Win | A number that stings, A character |
| Story | Odd pair, Life moment, Confession |
| How-To | Famous name, Short maxim, Warning |
| 9-Word Email | Their first name, Question, Plain label |
| Event Invite | Plain label (email 1), Question (email 3) |

## Too long? Cut to the hook

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| What happens to your marketing if you take next week off? (57) | Take next week off? (19) |
| I had to close the doors early for my last workshop (51) | I closed the doors early (24) |
| I posted for two years before I understood any of this (54) | Two years of posting, zero leads (32) |
| Your business shouldn't need five tools to run your marketing (61) | Five tools is four too many (27) |

These four are Danny's real subject lines, before and after.

## Make them yours

Paste five subject lines from your own inbox that made you open, into the Email Room (Step 1) or your EMAIL-BRAIN.md under "Subject lines I love". From then on, every subject line follows that style: the length, the tone, the pattern. Never the exact words.
